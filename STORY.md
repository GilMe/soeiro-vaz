# Soeiro Vaz na Terra da Pimenta — story bible

**Logline.** A man who publicly bet against Vasco da Gama spends the day the
fleet comes home trying to sell eleven tons of the wrong pepper.

---

## Why 1499 Lisbon is a story about now

The setting is not decoration. Every load-bearing element maps onto something
a player is living through, and the mapping is what the comedy runs on.

| 1499 | today |
|---|---|
| The India route makes a hundred men rich in one morning | The boom you didn't get into |
| Malagueta — the African pepper — collapses overnight | Cornering the substitute right before the real thing gets cheap |
| Rents in the Ribeira triple because the money came home | Priced out of your own city by money that isn't yours |
| Diogo got rich by getting on a boat | Watching someone mediocre win on timing |
| He said it in the tavern and the tavern remembers | Being permanently, publicly wrong |
| He works at the trading house, writing other men's fortunes | Adjacent to enormous wealth, receiving none of it |
| Eleven tons he pays to store and cannot sell | Sunk cost you can't admit is dead |

The rule: **the behaviour is period-accurate, the register is modern.** No
anachronistic vocabulary. Merchants really did talk like this. It just happens
to sound exactly like a man with a pitch deck.

---

## Soeiro Vaz, 31

A clerk at the crown's trading house who does not consider himself a clerk. He
considers himself early.

He told everyone Gama would die at sea. He had done the arithmetic. So he
borrowed 4,000 *reais* from Bastião Ruivo and bought every grain of malagueta in
Lisbon, on the reasoning that when the fleet failed he would own the only pepper
in Europe.

His signboard reads **COMPANHIA REAL DA MALAGUETA**. It is not royal and it is
not a company. It is one man and a shed.

**He has never said he was wrong.** His line, in every act:

> *"O mercado ainda não estava preparado."* — "The market wasn't ready."

Other things he says instead of the truth: the debt is *capital de risco*, the
shed is *a operação*, strangers are *sócio*, and he is not unemployed, he is
*entre projectos*.

**The flaw is the plot.** He cannot admit error, so he pivots. Every pivot
solves the last problem by creating a bigger one.

## The cast

**Bastião Ruivo** — the creditor. Never appears. His man leans in the alley
instead, patient, folding his hands. The threat is that he is in no hurry.

**Diogo** — the cousin. Not clever. Got on a boat. Soeiro condescended to him
for it and now has to watch. Diogo's India stories are the currency Soeiro is
short of.

**Dona Brízida** — the landlady. Has tripled the rent, without malice, because
a man from Alfama offered double and *"a Ribeira agora é outra coisa."* She is
not a villain. She is the market.

**A peixeira** — the fishwife on the Ribeira. Thirty years selling sardines. No
growth strategy, no five-year plan, not scaling, entirely fine. She is the only
person in the game who is not inside the bubble and the only one who treats
Soeiro like a person rather than a pitch. **She is the counter-argument to his
entire worldview, and she is kind to him.** Protect this.

---

## Structure — four pivots

1. **A Posição.** Worthless stock, tripled rent, a creditor with time on his
   hands. Pivot: sell the malagueta *as* Indian pepper.
2. **O Rebranding.** The right sacks, the right smell, and paperwork — he is a
   clerk, he can forge a manifest. It works. Then a buyer asks for volume.
3. **A Ronda.** To supply volume he raises money against a cargo that does not
   exist. Now he owes everyone, not only Ruivo.
4. **A Saída.** The only way to produce a real cargo is to fetch one. Cabral's
   fleet sails in March 1500 and he has to be on it.

**Ending.** Below deck, seasick, pitching his next venture to a sailor who wants
him to stop talking. Above his head the fleet swings west and makes landfall on
a coast nobody was expecting — Brazil, April 1500. The largest thing to happen
to Portugal in a century happens forty feet away, and the man who bets on the
future misses it because he is being sick into a bucket.

## Mechanics that carry the theme

**The debt counter.** Beside the score. Sierra's score goes up when you win;
Soeiro's debt goes up faster. Every clever escape adds to it. By Act 4 the
number is absurd and the ship is visibly the only door left. The player *feels*
the trap close instead of being told about it.

**Rent rises every act.** Announced by Dona Brízida, apologetically.

**Storage fees.** The warehouse charges daily for eleven tons he cannot sell.
Sunk cost, mechanised.

**The tavern remembers.** NPCs greet him with his own prediction:
*"Ó Soeiro! Então e o Gama? Morreu no mar?"* This never stops.

**The pitch lock.** Dialogue options are always pitch-speak. He is structurally
incapable of saying a plain sentence — the option is not offered. **Exactly once,
late, a plain option appears: he can tell the truth and say he is frightened.**
Taking it costs him the deal. That is the whole game in one choice, and it is
the moment people will remember.

---

## Tone

Funny first. The ache underneath is the reason the jokes land, not a substitute
for them. Never sentimental — Soeiro would not permit it, and the fishwife would
not either.
